Methods and limitations

How this resource separates evidence from interpretation, how the reflection lists were built, and what they can and cannot tell you.

Editorial commitments

  • Distinguish observational associations from causal conclusions, and say which is which.
  • Specify when findings come from occupations other than teaching.
  • Distinguish documented organizational behavior from employees' perceptions of it.
  • Do not treat contested terms such as moral injury as interchangeable with clinical diagnoses.
  • Hold student rights and educator well-being as simultaneous concerns.
  • Where the research is thin, say so rather than filling the gap with opinion.
  • Describe conditions and responses, never the reader's character.

Evidence labels

Every entry in the research library carries one of three labels. Direct: research on teachers, about the relationship being discussed. Adjacent: research from other occupations or on a related outcome. Theory: a framework or definition, not an empirical finding about teachers. Claims on the reflection page that go beyond what any source establishes are flagged in the text rather than given a library entry.

How sources were chosen

The library is a curated set of 21 sources, not a systematic review. Priority went to meta-analyses and large or longitudinal teacher samples for empirical claims, to the originating papers for definitions and frameworks, and to the most recent national survey data for prevalence. Each source was checked against its publisher record before inclusion.

How the reflection lists were built

The reflection page has two lists. The conditions list (19 statements in five groups) was written from the working conditions the library ties to burnout: time pressure and load, decision latitude, institutional response to mistreatment, value conflict, and recognition, fairness, and support. Each group sits under the evidence it draws on. The cost list (9 statements in three groups) is a set of plain-language descriptions of the three burnout dimensions in the WHO and Maslach accounts: exhaustion, mental distance, and reduced efficacy. The statements are original phrasings written for this page. They are not items from the Maslach Burnout Inventory or any other licensed instrument, and they have not been validated against one.

The readings are produced from simple counts. Each list is divided into thirds: fewer than a third of its statements marked reads as low, a third to two-thirds as moderate, more than two-thirds as high. The combined reading averages the two proportions and applies the same thirds. When one list is high and the other low, the page adds a sentence about the gap, because the mismatch is more informative than either count alone. The thresholds were chosen for clarity, not derived from data, and the page says so where the result is shown.

What the lists can do is show a reader, in one place, which of the conditions the research has studied are present for them and how much the job is currently costing them. What they cannot do is measure burnout, predict anything, or distinguish burnout from any other state. The page is built so that the reader interprets their own result; it offers readings, not conclusions.

Current limitations

  • No systematic search was conducted; relevant work has certainly been missed.
  • Study quality was assessed only at the level of design, not through formal appraisal.
  • The teacher evidence on autonomy and value conflict comes mainly from Norwegian and Finnish samples; U.S. evidence is mostly prevalence data.
  • No included study tests whether fear of complaints or liability changes teacher behavior, and none measures institutional betrayal in a school workforce.
  • The organizational-versus-individual intervention comparison rests on physician studies and has not been replicated in schools.
  • The proposed pathway from sustained conditions through helplessness and moral distress to burnout, and from burnout toward depression, is supported link by link as association, not as a sequence.
  • The reflection lists are unvalidated and their thresholds are arbitrary by design.

Privacy

The reflection runs entirely in the browser. Nothing a reader marks is stored, transmitted, or logged. The site makes no external requests.

Corrections and updates

The site is plain HTML intended for version control, so that changes to the evidence base can be tracked. Version 0.3, revised October 16, 2026.
