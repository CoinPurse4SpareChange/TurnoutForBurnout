Research library

Direct links to primary sources and explicit notes on what each source can—and cannot—establish.

Entries below provide stable links, study types, and scope limits. This is a curated starting set, not a systematic review.

R1. Aloe, A. M., Shisler, S. M., Norris, B. D., Nickerson, A. B., & Rinker, T. W. (2014). A multivariate meta-analysis of student misbehavior and teacher burnout. Educational Research Review, 12, 30–44.

Meta-analysis

21 independent samples; student misbehavior associated with burnout dimensions. Observational findings do not determine causality.

Open publication ↗

R2. Maier, S. F., & Seligman, M. E. P. (2016). Learned helplessness at fifty: Insights from neuroscience. Psychological Review, 123(4), 349–367.

Theory / neuroscience review

Controllability research; much mechanistic evidence comes from animal experiments. Do not equate occupational stress directly with laboratory conditions.

Open publication ↗

R3. Bakker, A. B., Demerouti, E., & Sanz-Vergel, A. (2023). Job demands–resources theory: Ten years later. Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior, 10, 25–53.

Research review

Job demands and resources, including autonomy and social support, are central to understanding burnout and engagement.

Open publication ↗

R4. World Health Organization. (2019). Burn-out an occupational phenomenon: International Classification of Diseases.

Authoritative definition

Burnout is an occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition; it includes exhaustion, job-related cynicism/distance, and reduced efficacy.

Open publication ↗

R5. Grandey, A. A., Dickter, D. N., & Sin, H.-P. (2004). The customer is not always right: Customer aggression and emotion regulation of service employees. Journal of Organizational Behavior, 25, 397–418.

Service-work study

Customer aggression and emotional regulation among service workers; adjacent-sector evidence, not a direct teacher study.

Open publication ↗
