Autonomy and disempowerment

Why responsibility without decision-making power can be psychologically costly.

What autonomy means

Professional autonomy is not the right to act without oversight. It involves meaningful discretion within appropriate legal, ethical, and institutional boundaries. The job demands–resources model recognizes that demands and resources jointly shape occupational well-being. [R3]

What repeated uncontrollability may do

Maier and Seligman revisited learned helplessness in light of neuroscience, emphasizing the importance of learning that one's actions affect outcomes. Their mechanisms cannot be directly imported from experimental animal settings into schools, but the theory gives us useful questions about perceived control. [R2]

Important distinction: Low autonomy, learned helplessness, and clinical depression are not synonyms. A teacher may face genuine barriers rather than hold an inaccurate belief that action is impossible.

A responsibility–authority gap

Ask which expectations are imposed on the educator, what actions they may take, and whether the institution will support justified decisions after complaints or incidents. These dimensions should be assessed separately.
